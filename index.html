Research

Statistical methods for lifetime and reliability data

My research is methodology-driven and motivated by applications in reliability and biomedical time-to-event data. I develop statistical methods for censored and dependent lifetime data, with particular emphasis on robust inference, stochastic orders, order-restricted inference, survival models, and the reliability of complex systems.

01

Survival Analysis & Biostatistics

Censoring, cure and frailty models, competing risks, transformation models, mean residual life, and modern computational methods.

02

Reliability & Stochastic Models

Coherent systems, redundancy allocation, component importance, ageing, mixtures, and dependent lifetime models.

Robust Statistical Inference

Divergence-based inference, generalized likelihood, spacing-based methods, contamination, and robust Bayesian procedures.

Order-Restricted Inference

Stochastic comparisons, shape constraints, hazard and ageing orders, and constrained estimation and testing.

Current research projects

Methodological directions

Ongoing work spans robust inference, modern survival modelling, shape constraints, and the reliability of complex systems.

Survival

Robust inference under censoring

Robust estimation and testing for censored lifetime data, with attention to restricted-time functionals and resistance to contamination.

Computation

Gaussian-process and deep survival models

Flexible predictive models for complex time-to-event data while retaining uncertainty quantification and interpretable lifetime summaries.

Constraints

Stochastic-order-constrained inference

Estimation and testing under hazard, cumulative-hazard, mean-residual-life, and related stochastic-order restrictions.

Systems

Reliability of coherent systems

Performance measures, component allocation, ageing, and comparison for coherent and random weighted system structures.

Foundations

Generalized likelihood and divergence-based inference

Stable estimators and test statistics developed through divergence measures, generalized likelihood ideas, and sample-spacing methods.
